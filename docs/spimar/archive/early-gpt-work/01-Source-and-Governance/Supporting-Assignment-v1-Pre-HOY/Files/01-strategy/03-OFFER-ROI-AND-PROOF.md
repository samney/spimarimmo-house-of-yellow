## 3. Package presentation rules

- Explain who each package is for.
- Show deliverables, not only feature names.
- Distinguish included, optional and unavailable items.
- Expose capacity or deadlines only when operationally accurate.
- Use `Demander une proposition` if prices vary by edition.
- Never use a fake “most popular” badge.
- Keep the detailed legal/commercial conditions in the proposal, not hidden in UI.

## 4. ROI framework

The website should help the decision-maker model value without guaranteeing results.

### Inputs

- participation fee;
- travel/team/logistics cost;
- projects and inventory represented;
- visitor registrations matching target criteria;
- meetings booked/completed;
- qualified leads received;
- response rate;
- opportunity rate;
- reservation/sale rate;
- average contribution margin;
- brand/content value, reported separately.

### Commercial return

```text
Attributed gross contribution
= Attributed completed sales × average gross contribution per sale
```

```text
Event ROI
= (Attributed gross contribution − total event investment)
   / total event investment
```

Only calculate with an agreed attribution window and method.

### Funnel model

```text
Registrations
→ verified profiles
→ physical check-ins
→ exhibitor interactions
→ qualified leads
→ accepted opportunities
→ reservations
→ completed sales
```

Every stage needs a definition. Never substitute one stage for another.

## 5. What “qualified” must mean

The qualification model should define:

- contact validity;
- country/city of residence;
- intended Moroccan city;
- property type;
- budget band;
- purchase horizon;
- financing need;
- purpose: residence, secondary home, return, retirement or investment;
- consent to share with relevant exhibitors;
- duplicate and spam handling.

SPIMAR should publish a concise explanation of the method without exposing sensitive
operational controls.

## 7. Reporting product

The website should preview a sanitized sample report containing:

- campaign sources;
- registration and qualification;
- attendance/check-ins;
- appointments;
- lead delivery;
- engagement by project/city;
- content delivery;
- post-event status;
- lessons and next actions.

This transforms “reporting” from a claim into a tangible deliverable.

## 8. Before/during/after measurement

### Before

- campaign reach and clicks;
- landing conversion;
- qualified registration rate;
- appointment requests;
- target-segment distribution.

### During

- check-ins;
- stand interactions where measurable;
- appointments completed;
- conference/content engagement;
- issues and operational SLA.

### After

- lead acceptance;
- time to first contact;
- opportunity progression;
- attributable reservations/sales;
- satisfaction;
- renewal intent.

## 9. Evidence publication ladder

From strongest to weakest:

1. Audited/CRM-backed result with methodology.
2. Approved internal event report.
3. Named case study with evidence.
4. Named video testimonial.
5. Approved logo and participation fact.
6. Documentary photo/video.
7. General claim.

The homepage should rely on levels 1–5. General claims cannot carry the sales case.
