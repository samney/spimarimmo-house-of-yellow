## 2. Primary decision-maker personas

### Executive leadership / DG

**Job**

> Decide whether an international exhibition is a credible use of budget and
> management attention.

**Needs**

- strategic market access;
- operator credibility;
- cost and risk clarity;
- measurable commercial outcome;
- evidence that the format can support brand and sales.

**Likely objections**

- “How do I know the audience is qualified?”
- “What will this generate beyond visibility?”
- “Why not run our own campaign?”
- “What happened for similar developers?”
- “How are results measured after the event?”

### Commercial director

**Job**

> Build a pipeline of prospects that the sales team can follow and convert.

**Needs**

- visitor intent and budget signals;
- appointment process;
- lead format and ownership;
- response-time expectations;
- project/city fit;
- post-event handover and reporting.

**Likely objections**

- “Will I receive usable, deduplicated leads?”
- “Can meetings be pre-booked?”
- “How many prospects match my projects?”
- “What support does my sales team receive?”
- “How quickly are leads delivered?”

### Marketing director

**Job**

> Extend brand and project visibility into target diaspora markets with measurable
> campaign performance.

**Needs**

- campaign channels and targeting;
- media deliverables;
- brand placements;
- content production;
- attribution;
- reporting and reusable assets.

**Likely objections**

- “What campaign inventory is included?”
- “What reach is owned versus paid?”
- “Which content and interviews will we receive?”
- “How is our brand differentiated from other exhibitors?”
- “Can I measure each channel?”

### Event/project manager

**Job**

> Deliver the stand and team presence without operational surprises.

**Needs**

- timeline and checklist;
- stand specifications;
- shipping/installation rules;
- venue and access information;
- contacts and escalation;
- deadlines and approvals.

**Likely objections**

- “Who manages installation?”
- “What do we need to provide?”
- “What are the venue restrictions?”
- “What happens if content or materials are late?”

## 3. Partner personas

### Bank or financing partner

- acquire qualified financing conversations;
- gain brand authority with MRE;
- integrate advisers into the visitor journey;
- obtain measurable meeting and application outcomes.

### Sponsor

- obtain category exclusivity or high-visibility placement;
- access developer and investor networks;
- receive stage, media and content inventory;
- measure brand and engagement delivery.

### Institution/media partner

- support trustworthy market information;
- increase reach and legitimacy;
- participate in conferences and content;
- obtain clear accreditation and usage rights.

## 4. Visitor personas

### Concrete buyer

- compare verified projects;
- meet developers and banks directly;
- reduce uncertainty about buying from abroad;
- obtain clear next steps.

### Early-stage explorer

- understand cities, budgets, financing and process;
- decide whether to attend;
- save relevant information without immediate pressure.

### Investor/portfolio buyer

- compare yield, location and project credibility;
- access market/legal/financial expertise;
- arrange focused meetings.

### Return or retirement planner

- evaluate future main or secondary residence;
- understand financing, delivery and family considerations;
- build trust before a long-term decision.

## 5. Objection-to-proof matrix

| Objection | Required answer | Strongest proof |
|---|---|---|
| Audience quality | Explain qualification fields and validation | Methodology + profile distribution |
| Attendance scale | Separate registered, confirmed and checked-in | Check-in report by edition |
| Commercial value | Show meetings, leads and pipeline definitions | Case study with reporting period |
| Brand visibility | Show exact media deliverables | Campaign report and asset examples |
| International capability | Show successful editions and local operations | Event archive + local partners |
| Execution risk | Explain responsibilities and timeline | Before/during/after plan |
| Lead ownership | Define handover, consent and access | Lead-delivery policy |
| Follow-up | Show SLA and reporting cadence | Sample report |
| Price | Compare package inclusions and outcomes | Approved commercial brochure |
| Credibility | Show real people, brands and media | Approved logos, testimonials and press |

## 6. Page personalization without fragmentation

The global site can adapt emphasis using query/campaign context:

- developer campaign → exhibitor proof and relevant market;
- sponsor campaign → sponsorship inventory;
- bank campaign → financing-partner benefits;
- visitor campaign → direct route to the country edition.

Do not fork the codebase. Use CMS-defined audience variants with shared components and
analytics.

## 7. Content tone

### Use

- precise;
- commercial;
- confident;
- evidence-led;
- international;
- operationally credible;
- concise around claims.

### Avoid

- unsupported superlatives;
- vague “premium/luxury” language;
- generic institutional history before value;
- promises of guaranteed sales;
- visitor-only emotional messaging on B2B pages;
- excessive technical language for executive audiences.

## 8. Research plan

Before final copy, interview:

- 2–3 past exhibitors;
- 1 commercial director;
- 1 marketing director;
- 1 SPIMAR sales owner;
- 1 event-operations owner;
- 1 lead/reporting owner;
- 3–5 visitors from different intent levels.

Validate:

- actual purchase objections;
- words used for visitor quality;
- decision criteria for event budget;
- acceptable evidence;
- package-comparison needs;
- post-event frustrations;
- brochure and meeting expectations.
